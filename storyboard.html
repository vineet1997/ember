One Ember

A scroll-driven film of the peopling of the habitable world — 300,000 BP to roughly 1300 CE, when the last major landmass was settled, then a bridge across the last seven hundred years. Fourteen beats, four acts. If a shot isn't specified here, it isn't in the film. Every date is bound to timeline.json; every coastline, ice margin, sea level and sky colour is read from data/, never authored.

The thesis

Every one of us is African. One line that left did not stop, and its descendants now live everywhere else.

Humanity is the protagonist. The earth is the antagonist — not a backdrop, an opponent with moves: it freezes, it erupts, it drops the sea and then takes the road away. The dramatic question is planted in Act I and answered in beat 05: we had tried to leave before and failed every time, so what made the last one different?

Every beat below either serves that question or corrects something the reader arrived believing. Nothing is here to be pretty.

Corrected. The previous thesis read "everyone alive descends from a few thousand people who walked out of Africa and did not stop." That is literally false — it makes Africans spectators in a story supposedly about everyone — and the data file already said so: beat 05's evidence reads all non-African ancestry. The copy over-claimed against its own data. See Law 07.

Seven laws

Fixed. Art, code and copy are checked against these, and a beat that needs an exception is a beat that needs rewriting. Law 03 was widened and Law 07 added after review.

LAW 01

One number governs everything

Deep time is a single float t, driven by scroll. Sea level, ice, sky, camera and every ember read t and nothing reads anything else. No subsystem may depend on another's state. This is what lets the film hold twelve layers without becoming fragile, and it means scrubbing backwards is always correct.

Asserted, not assumed. The spike hashes the full state at 401 points scrubbing forward, then again backward, and diffs them. It passes — and it kept passing through a complete renderer rewrite. Every sea depth on this page is read from the curve; seven changed the day the real data landed.

LAW 02

Humans are the only warm light

The earth is slate, ice, bone, deep blue. People are ember. Nothing else on screen is warm — not the sun, not the interface. Consequence: the film needs no legend and almost no labels.

Migration is light that travels. The spike shipped lights that simply appeared at their beat and sat there, and it read as a scatter of pins rather than a story. A population is a head that moves along a path with a trail behind it. Thirteen routes now live in the data for exactly this.

LAW 03 · two verbs

Three registers, two transitions

Planetary atlas when simultaneity matters. Orbital for movement. Ground for a moment.

Match cut for orbital ↔ ground — a shape held across a scale change. Unroll for orbital ↔ atlas — the camera pulls back and the globe flattens.

The spike proved the single-verb version wrong. A hard cut between projections has nothing to match on, because it is not a scale change — it is a change of space. It read as a glitch. Measured: from an Africa-centred globe only 2 of 6 agricultural centres are visible; the atlas shows 6 of 6.

LAW 04 · amended

The dot is a population, the cut is the evidence

Orbital is always about flows, never individuals — and orbital is then. Ground is always one named thing, and ground is now: the fossil, the bead, the footprint, as we hold it today. Only the orbital layer rides the time axis, so a ground cut can never break Law 01 — a 90,000-year-old fragment can be exhibited at any beat. Vastness, then a face.

LAW 05

Time is non-linear and must confess it

Ninety per cent of what the reader cares about sits in the last five per cent of the span. Silent compression is a lie, so a persistent ruler visibly stretches toward the present. Watching the ruler deform is itself an awe moment — it is the film telling you how recent you are.

LAW 06 · widened in Phase 5

Nothing on screen that isn't causal — for what happened, or for how we know it

Volcanoes, animals and weather appear only where they change what happens next. Toba dims the sky. The mammoth steppe is why Beringia was survivable. Anything else is decoration, and decoration is the entire distance between stunning and busy.

Resolved 2026-09-07, and the law widened rather than the eruption being cut. The Campanian Ignimbrite changes nothing about what happened — but at ±140 years it is the best-dated tephra horizon in European prehistory, which makes it how we know when beat 07 happened. The film already built a register for that and shipped it: beat 06 draws Madjedbebe's contested 65 ka reading on the time rail, not on the map, exactly so a claim about when cannot move the world. So the grammar exists, is enforced by the copy check, and works.

The widening is not a loosening, because it arrives with a bar the old law never had, and the bar is machine-checked rather than promised: an epistemic object may not perturb stateFor(t). The eruption may be a marker and a horizon and a sentence. It may not grey the sky, dim a light, or move the camera. The law06 test samples every world channel across the eruption's instant at two resolutions and fails on anything that does not behave like a continuous function — a jump does not shrink when you halve the sample spacing. Under the old law the CI was cut for a staging — "it greyed the sky exactly as their light failed" — that no test would have caught. This one catches exactly that staging.

LAW 08 · from the spike

Change is an edge that moves, never a fill that fades

Three of the spike's five failures were one bug. The emerging shelf, the advancing ice and the ancestral fan were all drawn as soft gradients, and all three were invisible — one of them so completely that the reviewer didn't notice anything was happening.

The eye tracks edges. A gradient says atmosphere; an edge says something is happening. And this film's whole subject is boundaries crossing the earth — a coastline, an ice margin, the leading edge of a population. Every one of them gets a visible line that moves.

LAW 07 · new

Copy is checked against the data, not just dates

Three separate failures shared one cause. The data said all non-Africans and the copy said everyone. The data said ~2% in non-Africans and the copy said two percent of you. The data said do not assert an extinction and the frame emptied the map. Numbers were being checked; sentences were not. Every on-screen line now gets diffed against the qualifiers its own event carries.

Pacing

Prescribed, not measured. Motion is how much moves on screen; weight is how much the beat carries. The rule the curve encodes: never two spectacles back to back — every peak is followed by a drop, and the largest emotional beat in the film has the least motion in it. Beat 10 is now split across its two frames (f1 holds at zero, f2 runs at five), which gives the film a second hold without spending a beat on one.

How to read a beat

Every card carries the same eight slots in the same order. A slot left empty is a decision not yet made, not a slot that didn't apply.

Frame
What is composed in the shot. Landscape, ~1440×900, type living beside the globe rather than on it.
Camera
Register (atlas / orbital / ground) and the move. Rotation is scroll-driven and damped at 0.08.
Earth
Sea level and temperature, both read from data/, plus ice and sky. All functions of t.
Cut in / out
How you enter and leave. match = a shape held across a scale change; hold = no cut, the camera stops.
On screen
The actual film copy, verbatim. Ceiling of roughly twenty words per frame. If it needs more, it is two beats.
Sound
Additive only. Nothing may be communicated in audio alone — every hold must survive mute.
Evidence
The anchors. solid = well dated. dashed = contested — the same grammar the film uses for routes.
Why it's here
Its job in the story, and the one thing the reader leaves with. A beat that cannot fill this gets cut.

The line that held

What carries a hold
The question the beat was built to answer, and the answer is: not the camera. The earth's own curve, which turns out to have a three-act shape sitting inside the lock for free.

This beat's subject is the negation of a migration: a light that is not going anywhere and is still lit. So the plume crosses in the first half of the lock, then stops and stays, and the staying is the sentence. Any other beat doing this would be breaking the law.

Absence, as a function of t
BUILT, Phase 5, and this was the beat's real problem. A flag would do it in one line and would be wrong — a flag is a memory of a t you passed through, and Law 01 says the frame is a function of the t you are at. Scrub backwards past a flag and the colour never comes back; jump to 0.9 without passing 0.44 and it never leaves.

Instead paleRGB(t) returns the colour when it exists and null when it does not — and null is not a dimmed colour, it is nothing to draw with. Every site that draws pale light reads it and returns early, so the palette is not policed, it is absent. The absence test walks t from the extinction out to 1.0 — across beats 08 to 14, which do not exist yet — and proves the colour is null at all 3,001 samples, then walks it backwards and proves it returns at the same t to five places. Being able to test seven unbuilt beats today is the whole return on one continuous t.
How the light goes
The pale population occupies; it does not travel. Routes are the wrong shape for that, so it gets a field — 210 points on land inside an indicative ring, drawn diffuse because its confidence is band and a hard outline would claim a frontier no source supports.

The patches shrink. They do not fade. Law 08 forbids the fade, and a field of lights dimming together would also read as one population getting quieter, when what Higham describes is range contracting in a mosaic. Radius to zero is an edge that moves.

And it does not contract toward a last refuge, because there is no sourced last refuge and drawing one would invent the most memorable fact in the beat. The order patches go out is fixed noise, baked into the data file, carrying no dates, and the frame says EXTENT INDICATIVE · NOT A SOURCED RANGE MAP.
The idea that was cut
The pale light was going to be absorbed into the ember as it went — the 2% entering us, literally drawn. It is the prettiest idea in the beat and it is an assertion that Neanderthals ended by absorption, which is one of three contested causes. So it is cut, and the 2% is stated in type, where it can carry its qualifier, instead of in the picture, where it would have picked a winner. The copy check now asserts the absorption visual is absent.

Four places the diagnosis was good and the prescription wasn't.

The renderer is a fragment shader

Not a stylistic preference — the data forced it, and it makes the film simpler rather than harder.

Projecting a thresholded raster onto a globe needs per-pixel inverse projection. That is a shader's job; canvas cannot do it at frame rate. So the visual engine is a fullscreen quad and one fragment shader, exactly the shape described in craft/webgl-shaders.md.

What that buys: the film needs no coastline geometry at all. No vector data, no simplification pass, no per-beat cartography. One elevation raster, one float, one comparison — and every shoreline in every beat falls out of it, including the ones nobody has drawn because they have been underwater for twelve thousand years.

What the atlas found

Phase 5, step 2. Beat 06 as a static artifact — eight stills baked from the film's own fragment shader at eight values of t, with every word placed back onto them as live HTML. It is the mobile experience, the reduced-motion fallback, the no-JS fallback, the crawler content and the answer for a browser that has switched WebGL off, and it is one file rather than five. Again, only the things that changed a law, a number or a frame.

  • DecisionBake the world. Never bake a word. The earth, the lights, the leaders and the marker dots come out of the shader as pixels, because there is no honest way to reproduce a shader in a static page. Type does not. The atlas is the crawler content, the screen-reader fallback and the page a browser without WebGL receives — baked type would be pixels in exactly the artifact whose job is to be text, and it would be invisible to the Law 07 copy check, which reads the film's own surfaces.

    So film.js grew an annotation sink. No canvas text site writes a glyph any more; all three hand their string to one function that either draws it (the film) or records where it would have gone (the bake), in CSS pixels, with the class the type system gives it. The bake emits that geometry beside each still, and the atlas puts real text on the end of a leader the still has already drawn. And the claim is checked on the functions, not on the output: drawLabels, drawGap and drawCut are asserted to contain no fillText, so a glyph put back onto the canvas fails a test on the day it is typed.
  • FoundFive on-screen lines had never been in the copy check, and had not been since Phase 4. The ground register's instrumentation — GROUND REGISTER · PRESENT TENSE, ORIGINAL DIAGRAM, NOT A PHOTOGRAPH OF THE SITE, and the reserved plate's three lines — were string literals inside a draw call, and the check reads tables. Two of the five make claims the film is answerable for: that the stencil is a diagram rather than a photograph, and that a plate is reserved rather than missing. They are a table now, and they are checked.

    The check could not see them, which is the same failure as the check that only worked once, wearing different clothes. The copy check went from 30 lines to 44 in this step, and five of the fourteen are lines that were already on screen.
  • PassedA sequence of stills is beat 06, and the reason has nothing to do with the page being well made. It is a property of the beat. Beat 06 is a journey, so its content is change of place: take any two frames and something is somewhere else — the light, the coastline, the camera — and the frames in order are the beat. That is the grammar an atlas has, and here it is the beat's grammar too.
  • OpenAnd it does not generalise. The two beats either side are temporal devices, and a page cannot spend time. This is the finding, and it is written down rather than solved.

    Beat 05's subject is duration. “Nothing happens for ten thousand years” is said by spending scroll on nothing: the camera is bit-identical across 0.060 of t, a quarter of the running artifact, and the beat's last fifth has only the year moving. A still of the dead stretch is a locked camera over a flat sea — correct, and it says nothing, because the content is the time the reader spent looking at it. Two stills of the same frame with different year labels is not duration; it reads as a mistake.

    Beat 07's subject is a subtraction that has to persist. “That colour never comes back” is a claim about every t after 0.4405, including seven beats that do not exist; absence() walks it out to 1.0 and proves it. A static page has no after. Print a frame with two lights and a frame with one and the reader sees a change — they cannot see permanence, because permanence is a property of the frames you did not print.

    So the atlas's grammar carries change of place and cannot carry duration or absence. Two candidate second ideas, neither prototyped, neither believed yet. For 05: invert the figure — one still, printed once, and the ruler becomes the illustration, with the three-act shape of the sea-level curve beside it, asking the reader to read a duration off an axis instead of feeling it. That is an infographic rather than a film, and it may be the honest thing a page that cannot spend time can do. For 07: a page can carry permanence only by stating it — and a claim stated has to be shown to be enforced, which means the evidence is absence()'s own result, printed. What is decided is the negative: do not extend this atlas to 05 and 07 by adding more stills. Eight more frames of a locked camera would be the atlas lying about what the beat is.
  • CorrectedA caption named a site the frame does not hold. Shot 06's caption talked about Liang Metanduno; at that t Metanduno projects off the bottom of the frame. Nothing in the sentence was false — it was doing the reader's imagining for them. It moved to shot 08, which is the Metanduno stencil and whose record voice is already that event, and shot 06 now says what its frame actually shows: Sahul as one continent, and the Madjedbebe ring marking a place while its contested years stay on the rail.
  • FailedThe copy check failed twice on the new lines, and both times the check was the thing that was wrong. That is now the third build running where fixing a failing check made it say something truer.

    One: for “the rocks argue for older” the check read madjedbebe.dispute.alternative, which is 50,000–47,000 — the younger reading, because that field holds the objection to the rocks. The older alternative is on sahul-arrival, whose dispute names 65,000–59,000. What the vertical slice found

  • MeasuredThe beat's central number was nearly wrong by a factor of fourteen. The first attempt measured coastline-to-coastline from Sunda to Sahul and got 994 km. That is a real distance and the wrong quantity. The claim is a bottleneck: over every island-hopping route, the smallest achievable longest hop. Computed properly — a Euclidean-Voronoi pass over the water to find which components are ever nearest neighbours, exact great-circle minima for those pairs, then union-find in ascending order — it is 70.5 km, and the floor across every sea level tested is 70.4 km. The sea falls 131 metres and the gap does not close. That is the beat.
  • MeasuredThe bottleneck hop lands on the northern corridor — Sula to Buru, about 126°E · 3°S, through the Spice Islands. That is the corridor the 2026 Liang Metanduno paper argues for.   • FailedTerrain-RGB is not mip-safe, and it cost most of a day. Elevation is stored as R×256 + G, so a mip level averages the high byte and rounds it — half a step of R is 39 metres. That averaging happens exactly where R changes, which is exactly at a coastline. On a shelf sloping a tenth of a metre per kilometre, 39 m moves the waterline hundreds of kilometres. The film now decodes the PNG to a float texture on load, which makes the pyramid valid and the shader shorter.
  • FailedLaw 08 needs a third clause: an edge is a pixel, not a metre. Un-smoothed, the ghost coastline came out as frost scattered over ten pixels rather than a line.

The rule, and what it cost — nothing on screen that isn't in the data, and nothing in the data without a date range, a confidence level and a citation. Enforcing it has restructured three beats, killed five lines of copy, added two laws and amended two more, corrected fifteen sea levels, rewritten the thesis, rebuilt the ending, and thrown away a renderer. Every one was a loss at the time and an improvement afterwards.

And the pattern underneath all of it — every serious error in this project was the same kind: a sentence that claimed more than the data behind it. The thesis said everyone where the data said non-Africans. The frame emptied a continent where the note said not to. The shelf was drawn as a fill where the physics is an edge. The data was almost always right and the prose almost always drifted. And a fifth kind has now happened twice, which makes it a pattern rather than an anecdote: a check that stops being able to see what it checks. The copy check once searched the whole page for a degree sign and found the sentence explaining the check. One phase later, a new check searched the whole page for a no-script element and found one the panel had built out of the sentence explaining that check. Both passed once and failed forever after. The lesson is not “be careful with wording” — it is that a check must be scoped to a surface a check's own output cannot enter, and that a panel which renders explanations as markup will keep manufacturing this bug. Both are fixed at the mechanism now.

The slice added a fourth of exactly that kind: the beat’s headline crossing distance was first computed as the wrong quantity — 994 km of coastline separation where the claim was a 70 km bottleneck — and it would have gone on screen looking perfectly measured. That is what Laws 07 and 08 exist to catch.

Beats 05 and 07 produced the same failure twice more, and both times the check caught it. A sentence read “between five and nine kilometres” when the strictest island threshold measures 9.1 — falsified by its own copy check before it reached a frame. And the first Law 06 test compared a channel’s step against its average rate rather than testing for a discontinuity, so it failed on a channel that was moving fast for its own reasons. Arithmetically fine, wrong quantity, twice. The lesson is holding: it is not enough to check the number, you have to check that the number is the sentence.
