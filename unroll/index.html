Does the shader agree with the overlay

The shader inverse-maps a pixel to a lon/lat; project() forward-maps a lon/lat to a pixel. They are two different pieces of code for one surface, and if they disagree the lights slide off the ground they are standing on. This reads pixels back off the GPU and diffs them against project().

Is k=0 still the film's globe

At k=0 the morph must be the unit sphere EXACTLY, not nearly - otherwise entering the unroll costs a pop. Compares surf() against the film's own xyz() at a few thousand points.

Law 01 — the morph is a function of t

Hashes the whole state scrubbing forward, then backward, and diffs. The unroll must be a pure function of t like everything else, or it cannot be scrubbed.

Law 07 — nothing on screen that is not in the record

Every place this beat lights has to be an event in timeline.json with its own coordinates, dates and citations, and the film voice has to be the beat's own onScreen field verbatim. The first version of this slice failed all of that and none of it would have shown in a frame.

Why the atlas register exists at all

Law 03's justification is a measurement: a globe cannot hold beat 12. Counts how many domestication centres are visible in this frame, at this k, and reports the count at both ends.
