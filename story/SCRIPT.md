# One Ember — master narration, draft 1

This is spoken narration, not display copy. It is written to leave air around the earth, the ember, and the ruler. Bracketed cues are intentional silence. Each factual passage resolves to the beat events listed in `narration.json`.

## Prologue

Before there were people, the earth was already in motion. Ice came and went. Seas moved across the land. Coastlines appeared, vanished, and appeared again.

[Opening globe — silence]

## Act I · Africa

### 01 · The dark earth

Three hundred thousand years ago, the world was already changing. The shorelines were not ours. The climate was not ours. There was no sign yet of the story that would one day connect this planet.

### 02 · Shimmer

Across Africa, groups of people lived far apart, yet remained connected across many generations. Our species did not begin in one place. It emerged from a continent.

### 03 · The failed doors

Some people left Africa long before the great dispersal. They reached new lands. They made homes. They buried their dead. But those journeys did not endure.

[Qafzeh burial — silence]

## Act II · The breakout

### 04 · Toba

Then, seventy-four thousand years ago, a volcano on Sumatra darkened skies far beyond the island. It was a catastrophe. But it did not bring humanity to the edge of extinction, as the old story once claimed.

### 05 · The line that held

Later, another dispersal began. It was not the first, and it was not guaranteed to succeed. But it endured. Along the way, these travellers met other human peoples and had children together. People with ancestry outside Africa today carry part of that journey.

[The sea edge moves; the camera holds — silence]

### 06 · The crossing to Sahul

Falling seas joined many islands to the continents around them. But the water east of Asia never became a bridge. The people who reached Sahul crossed open sea.

### 07 · The others

Other human peoples still lived across Eurasia. By the end of this beat, Neanderthals are gone. We do not know exactly why.

[The pale ember withdraws — silence]

## Act III · The ice

### 08 · The world gets smaller

As the planet cooled, ice spread across the north and the sea withdrew from the coasts. Still, people travelled into the cold. At Sungir, a child was buried with thousands of ivory beads, each one shaped by hand.

[Sungir detail — silence]

### 09 · Mammoth steppe

At the height of the ice age, Beringia was a vast grassland between Asia and North America. It was home, not simply a bridge to somewhere else.

[Wind over Beringia — silence]

### 10 · The sweep

Far to the south, footprints appeared in the mud of what is now New Mexico. They tell us that people were already there. After that, the evidence falls quiet for thousands of years.

[The ruler moves; the ember becomes uncertain — silence]

When the record becomes clear again, people are moving rapidly across two continents.

## Act IV · The return

### 11 · The doors close

Then the ice began to melt. The sea rose behind the people who had crossed into new lands. At the same time, some communities began to settle and cultivate the places where they lived. Beringia disappeared. Doggerland disappeared. The paths that had carried generations forward went under water.

[Rising water erases the routes — silence]

### 12 · Seeds

Across widely separated parts of the world, communities began to cultivate plants and reshape their landscapes. They did not learn this from one source. They found different ways of doing it for themselves.

[The globe opens into the atlas — silence]

### 13 · Sails

Once the seas had settled, people learned to travel them with growing confidence. Canoes crossed the Pacific and Indian Oceans. Other migrations spread across Africa, Europe, Asia, and the Atlantic. The movement never stopped. It changed its scale.

### 14 · You

In the last five hundred years, migration took on another form: empire, enslavement, expulsion, labour, and flight. Millions moved because they were forced to move.

Follow any family story far enough back, and it stops being a single line. It opens into a vast human web. Each of us carries a different path through the same deep past.

[The ancestral fan — final silence]
