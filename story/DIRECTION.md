# One Ember — directing specification

## The story

One Ember is the story of people making lives across deep time. Humanity does
not march inevitably across an empty map. Populations begin across Africa;
some early dispersals end; one later dispersal endures. People cross routes
that the earth sometimes opens and later removes. Their descendants reach the
habitable world through persistence, adaptation, encounter, loss, settlement,
navigation, coercion and time.

The story is **persistence and contingency**, never destiny. What happened was
not guaranteed to happen. The ember is not conquest or a chosen people; it is
the visual trace of human movement.

## The narrator

The voice is warm, patient, plain-spoken, and observational: natural-history
documentary rather than a trailer, manifesto, lecture, or slogan. It uses
complete sentences and concrete nouns. It says what the viewer needs to know
to understand the image, then leaves room for the image to be felt.

The narrator never:

- calls a historically contingent outcome inevitable;
- turns an evidence gap into an empty world;
- assigns a disputed cause as fact;
- makes African, mixed, diasporic, or non-African viewers spectators in their
  own human story;
- repeats information already made legible by the image.

## The screen

The earth shows changing conditions. The ember shows people. The ruler makes
time visible. The narration makes those moving images into a story.

Production UI contains only:

- a quiet beat/date marker at top left;
- the deep-time ruler at the bottom;
- occasional place or evidence labels when identification is needed;
- accessible two-line subtitles in the lower-left narration safe area.

Diagnostic controls, developer panels, and continuation links are not part of
the film surface. They remain available only in development or an explicit
accessibility/evidence layer.

The image arrives before speech. Narration generally enters about one second
after a new frame establishes itself and ends before a major cut. No subtitle
is present during an intentional silence.

## The four acts

| Act | Beats | Dramatic movement |
|---|---:|---|
| I · Africa | 01–03 | Many populations, early journeys, and the knowledge that leaving was not assured. |
| II · The breakout | 04–07 | Catastrophe, an enduring dispersal, open-sea crossing, encounter, and loss. |
| III · The ice | 08–10 | The planet at its harshest; care, waiting, uncertainty, then rapid spread. |
| IV · The return | 11–14 | Rising water closes old routes; new lifeways and navigation; forced movement; the shared human web. |

## Required silences

1. The opening globe before the first narration.
2. The Qafzeh burial in Beat 03.
3. Beat 05 after “But it endured.”
4. The pale ember’s withdrawal in Beat 07.
5. The Sungir detail after the accelerated ice sequence.
6. The long Beringia view in Beat 09.
7. The evidence gap in Beat 10; the ruler moves while certainty does not.
8. Rising water erasing routes in Beat 11.
9. The complete atlas reveal in Beat 12.
10. The final ancestral fan in Beat 14.

## Narrative contracts by beat

Every beat must answer one question and leave with one visual change. The
screenplay may use more than one spoken passage, but it may not violate these
contracts.

| Beat | Question | Visual change |
|---:|---|---|
| 01 | What existed before us? | A world without ember becomes a world ready for one. |
| 02 | How did our species begin? | One imagined origin becomes a continental pattern. |
| 03 | Was leaving inevitable? | Early routes fail; the ground cut makes their lives real. |
| 04 | Did catastrophe nearly end us? | Toba darkens the world without erasing people. |
| 05 | What made the later dispersal different? | One moving ember remains lit. |
| 06 | Was Sahul reachable by land? | The sea remains visible as the ember crosses it. |
| 07 | Who was already there? | Two human lights become one, without asserting why. |
| 08 | What did people do in the cold? | Planetary ice resolves into a made object and a burial. |
| 09 | What was Beringia? | A “bridge” becomes a lived-in country. |
| 10 | What can an evidence gap tell us? | A light becomes uncertain, never extinguished; later movement accelerates. |
| 11 | What happened to old routes? | Water erases them while settlement begins elsewhere. |
| 12 | Did cultivation have one source? | Separate lights become simultaneously visible in an atlas. |
| 13 | What changed when people learned to navigate? | Oceans become traversed networks. |
| 14 | How does this history reach us? | Individual lines widen into a shared ancestral web. |
