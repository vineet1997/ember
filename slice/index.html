The earth pass is the SHADER; the overlay is the 2D canvas. Everything Phase 5 added - the plume, the pale field - is overlay work, and the GPU timer cannot see any of it. Two numbers, because one of them would have been honest and useless.

Scroll for a few seconds first. The first row is a rolling two-second window; “worst while visible” keeps a one-time hitch visible without mistaking a backgrounded tab for a slow frame. One 40 ms hitch is felt, a slightly low average is not. Green under 20 ms at p95.

These are the one-time phases for a background terrain tile. On a GPU-capable browser, the source image is uploaded and expanded into float elevation by one GPU pass: no canvas readback reaches JavaScript. The displayed GPU times are issuing calls, not hidden GPU completion. An unusual browser falls back to the stated canvas/CPU phases.

On one visible Slow-4G reload, wait for the two background tiles. While bytes are still arriving, the tile/state row names the tile and the phase clocks remain blank by design; they begin at “waiting for an idle gap.” Read the completed clocks beside “worst while visible.” Do not optimise or chunk a phase until this says which phase is actually large.

Law 01 — one number governs everything

Hashes the full state at 401 points scrubbing forward, then again backward, and diffs them.

A REGRESSION CHECK, and worth naming as one. It shows that stateFor hands back the same world at the same t at the points it samples, and that the year never runs backwards. It cannot see between two samples, and it says nothing about whether anything downstream reads a value that is not in the state - a channel computed inside a draw call is invisible to it, which is exactly how the sky tint stayed outside the Law 06 walk for a phase.

Law 07 — copy checked against the data

Diffs every on-screen line against the qualifiers its own event carries in timeline.json.

It bounds the FILM, not the literature: it can show that a sentence claims no more than the record behind it, and it cannot tell you the record is right. Three of the last five failures here were the check being wrong rather than the copy, and each time fixing the check made it say something truer.

Law 06 — epistemic, not causal

The Campanian Ignimbrite is on screen because it is how we know when beat 07 happened, not because it did anything. This half shows that it is drawn, and that no world channel takes a STEP at its instant - which is the bug the film actually committed, when the eruption used to arrive as grey sky.

Continuity is not independence, and this test does not prove non-influence: a channel driven smoothly by the eruption passes every line of it. That is the test below.

Law 06 — take it out and nothing moves

Strips the eruption from all four surfaces it occupies — the record, the margin, the copy and the rail — and asserts the world is bit-identical at every t. The only test here that compares the film against a configuration that could have disagreed.

Permanent absence — a colour leaves the film

When the pale light goes out it must stay out for the remaining seven beats. Walks t to 1.0 — through beats that are not built — and then walks it backwards.

What it certifies is a property of THIS function: paleRGB is null at every sampled t after the extinction, and returns at the same t scrubbing back. It cannot promise that beats 08 to 14 will not light a second pale population by some other route. It promises that this one will not.

The only true hold

Beat 05 is the film's only locked camera. Counts the spans where the camera is bit-identical and fails on more than one.

Counted at the sample spacing, so a lock shorter than that is invisible to it. The definition took two tries: a hold is a locked camera ON THE WORLD, and the first version counted a camera nobody was looking through.

Time the draw path

A frame interval reads 16.7 ms whether the work took 1 ms or 15, so it says nothing about headroom. This draws six real shots 160 times each, synchronously, and divides. It blocks for about a second.
